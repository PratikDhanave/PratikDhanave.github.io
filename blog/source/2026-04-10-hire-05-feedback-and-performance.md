# Feedback and Performance

*Most people's experience of "performance management" is the dreaded annual review — a backward-looking, anxiety-inducing ritual that helps no one improve. That's a symptom of getting feedback wrong. Done well, feedback isn't a once-a-year verdict but an ongoing, growth-oriented conversation that helps people do their best work. The shift — from judgment to growth, from annual to continuous — is what separates performance management that develops people from performance management that just rates them. And feedback, given well, is a skill worth developing.*

**Feedback and performance** — helping people understand how they're doing and do their best work — is central to developing people. This post covers giving feedback well (building on the EQ series), the shift from judgment to growth, continuous feedback over annual reviews, and performance management done right. It builds on onboarding (feedback continues developing people after they're settled) and connects to the EQ series' feedback skills. Done well, feedback develops people; done badly, it just rates and demoralizes them.

## Giving feedback well

**Feedback** — telling people how they're doing so they can improve — is essential, and giving it *well* is a learnable skill (covered in the EQ series, applied here to developing people):

- **Feedback helps people improve.** The purpose of feedback is to *help people improve and grow* — telling them what's working (to continue) and what isn't (to change), so they can do better. Feedback is *information for growth*, and without it people can't improve (they don't know what to change). Feedback enables growth. Improvement needs feedback.
- **Give it with helpful intent, specifically, kindly.** Good feedback (from the EQ series) comes from *genuinely wanting to help*, is *specific* (about behaviors/work, not character), *timely*, and delivered with *care* (respectfully, considering how it lands). Feedback given to *help* (not to criticize or vent), specifically and kindly, is received far better and drives improvement. Give feedback to help, specifically and kindly. Care and candor together.
- **Both positive and constructive.** Good feedback includes *both* positive (recognizing what's working — which reinforces good behavior and motivates) *and* constructive (what to improve). Not just criticism (demoralizing) nor just praise (no growth) — *both*, honestly. Balanced feedback (positive and constructive) develops people. Recognize the good, address the gaps.
- **It's a learnable skill.** Giving feedback well is a *skill* (the EQ series) — learnable through the principles above (helpful intent, specificity, care, balance). Many give feedback poorly (vague, harsh, or avoided); doing it well is a *developable competence* central to developing people. Feedback is a learnable skill. Get good at it.

Giving feedback well — with helpful intent, specifically, kindly, and balanced (positive and constructive) — helps people improve, and it's a learnable skill (from the EQ series). Feedback is information for growth. But *how* feedback is framed — as judgment or growth — matters enormously.

## From judgment to growth

The crucial shift in feedback and performance is **from judgment to growth** — framing feedback as *helping people improve*, not *rating/judging* them:

- **Judgment-framing vs growth-framing.** Feedback/performance can be framed as *judgment* (rating people, verdicts on their worth — "here's your score") or *growth* (helping them improve — "here's how to get better"). The *growth* framing is far more *effective* (and humane) — it motivates improvement rather than defensiveness/anxiety. Judgment framing triggers defensiveness; growth framing enables improvement. Frame feedback as growth, not judgment. Help, don't just rate.
- **Judgment triggers defensiveness.** Feedback framed as *judgment* (evaluating the person, a verdict) triggers *defensiveness* and *anxiety* (people protect themselves, don't hear it, feel threatened) — undermining the improvement it should drive. Judgment-framing *backfires* (defensiveness blocks learning — the EQ self-regulation theme). Judgment triggers defensiveness, blocking growth. Verdicts make people defensive.
- **Growth-framing enables improvement.** Feedback framed as *growth* (helping you improve, on your side) is *received openly* (less defensiveness) and *drives improvement* (the point). Growth-framing (feedback as *help for getting better*) is what makes feedback actually *work* to develop people. Growth-framing makes feedback effective. Help enables hearing.
- **It connects to growth mindset.** This connects to the *growth mindset* (the EQ series) — treating ability as *developable*, so feedback is about *growing* (not fixed judgment). A growth-oriented feedback culture (feedback helps everyone improve) develops people far better than a judgment-oriented one (feedback rates and threatens). Growth-framing reflects a growth mindset. Ability is developable; feedback helps develop it.

The crucial shift is from judgment to growth — framing feedback as *helping people improve* (which enables open reception and drives improvement) rather than *rating/judging* them (which triggers defensiveness and anxiety, blocking growth) — reflecting a growth mindset. This growth-framing is what makes feedback effective. And feedback works best *continuously*, not annually.

## Continuous feedback over annual reviews

A key practice is **continuous feedback** — ongoing, timely feedback — over the traditional *annual review*, which is widely recognized as ineffective:

- **The annual review is a poor tool.** The traditional *annual performance review* (once-a-year, backward-looking evaluation) is *widely criticized as ineffective* — feedback comes *too late* to act on (months after the fact), it's *anxiety-inducing* (a high-stakes verdict), and once a year is *far too infrequent* to actually help people improve. Annual reviews are a poor primary feedback tool. Once-a-year is too rare and too late.
- **Continuous feedback is better.** *Continuous feedback* — *ongoing, timely* feedback (in the moment, regularly) — is *far more effective*: it's *timely* (act on it now, while relevant), *frequent* (regular course-correction and growth), and *lower-stakes* (ordinary, not a dreaded annual verdict). Continuous feedback *actually* helps people improve (unlike the annual review). Continuous feedback beats annual reviews. Regular and timely wins.
- **Feedback should be regular and normal.** The healthy pattern: feedback (both positive and constructive) is *regular and normal* (part of ongoing work), not a rare, high-stakes event. Making feedback *frequent and low-stakes* (an ordinary part of how the team works) is far more effective for growth. Make feedback regular and normal. Ongoing, not an event.
- **This is a real shift in practice.** Moving from *annual reviews* to *continuous feedback* is a real, valuable *shift* in how organizations handle performance — many are moving this way (recognizing annual reviews' failures). For engineers/leaders, giving *regular, timely* feedback (not saving it for a review) is the effective approach. Shift to continuous feedback. Regular over annual.

Continuous feedback (ongoing, timely, frequent, lower-stakes) is far more effective than the traditional annual review (too late, too infrequent, anxiety-inducing) at actually helping people improve — a real, valuable shift in how performance is handled. Feedback should be regular and normal. This reframes performance management overall.

## Performance management done right

Bringing it together, *performance management* — the broader system of managing and developing people's performance — done *right* is growth-oriented, continuous, and developmental:

- **Performance management is about developing performance.** *Performance management* (managing how people perform) done *right* is about *developing and enabling* good performance — helping people do their best work (through feedback, growth, support, clear expectations) — not just *evaluating/rating* them. Its goal is *better performance*, achieved by *developing* people. Performance management develops performance. Enable, don't just evaluate.
- **Clear expectations enable performance.** Good performance management starts with *clear expectations* — people knowing *what's expected* and what good looks like (so they can meet it, and feedback has a reference). Clarity about expectations enables performance (people can't perform well against unclear expectations). Set clear expectations. Know the target.
- **Feedback and growth are the core.** The core of good performance management is *continuous, growth-oriented feedback* (above) plus *supporting growth* (development — the next post) — helping people *improve* and *do their best*. It's *developmental* (growing people) more than *judgmental* (rating them). Feedback and growth are the heart. Develop, don't just judge.
- **Address problems honestly (and fairly).** Performance management also means *addressing performance problems* honestly (when someone isn't meeting expectations) — through honest, growth-oriented feedback and support to improve, and, if that fails, fair difficult decisions. Even here, the approach is *honest and fair* (helping people improve where possible, handling it respectfully when not — the EQ hard-conversations skills). Address problems honestly and fairly. Handle issues with care.

Feedback and performance done right — giving feedback well (helpful, specific, kind, balanced), framed as *growth not judgment*, delivered *continuously* (not via ineffective annual reviews), within performance management that *develops* performance (clear expectations, feedback, growth, honest handling of problems) — is central to developing people and helping them do their best work. It's growth-oriented and humane, not just evaluative. Next: growth and career development.

## Key takeaways

- Feedback (telling people how they're doing so they can improve) is essential and a learnable skill (from the EQ series): give it with genuine helpful intent, specifically (about behaviors/work, not character), timely, kindly, and *balanced* (both positive — recognizing what works — and constructive) — feedback is information for growth.
- The crucial shift is from judgment to growth: framing feedback as *helping people improve* (which is received openly and drives improvement) rather than *rating/judging* them (which triggers defensiveness and anxiety, blocking learning) — reflecting a growth mindset (ability is developable), which is what makes feedback actually effective.
- Continuous feedback (ongoing, timely, frequent, lower-stakes) is far more effective than the traditional annual review (too late to act on, too infrequent to help, anxiety-inducing as a high-stakes verdict) — a real, valuable shift, making feedback regular and normal (part of ongoing work) rather than a rare dreaded event.
- Performance management done right is about *developing* performance (helping people do their best work) more than *evaluating* it — built on clear expectations (people knowing what good looks like), continuous growth-oriented feedback, and supporting growth (development).
- It also means addressing performance problems honestly and fairly (growth-oriented feedback and support to improve, and respectful difficult decisions if that fails — the EQ hard-conversations skills) — overall a growth-oriented, continuous, humane approach, not just backward-looking evaluation.
