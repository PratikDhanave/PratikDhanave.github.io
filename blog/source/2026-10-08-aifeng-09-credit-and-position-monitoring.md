# Credit and Position Monitoring — Watching the Thesis Over Time

*Underwriting is the decision to do a deal; monitoring is everything after — watching whether the thesis still holds as the world changes, the borrower performs or deteriorates, and covenants tighten or breach. It's traditionally periodic, manual, and uneven, which means problems get noticed late. AI makes monitoring continuous: reading every update, tracking every covenant, and flagging variance from the original thesis the moment it appears. This post is about building the monitoring layer.*

Post 8 covered the decision; this post covers the long aftermath, which in credit and private markets is *years* per position. Monitoring is where the risk actually plays out, and it's historically underserved — the attention goes into the underwriting decision and then fades. AI's ability to read continuously and tirelessly is transformative here, turning periodic manual review into continuous automated surveillance with humans focused on what changed. (Educational, not financial advice.)

## What monitoring is, and why it's hard

**Monitoring** is the ongoing surveillance of a position after the deal is done: tracking whether the investment/credit thesis (post 8) still holds as conditions evolve. In credit and private markets it involves:

- **Tracking performance against the thesis** — is the borrower/issuer performing as the underwriting assumed? Are revenues, margins, and leverage on track, or deteriorating?
- **Covenant compliance** — loans and bonds carry **covenants** (financial ratios the borrower must maintain, restrictions on their actions), and monitoring checks whether they're being met, how much *headroom* remains, and whether a breach is approaching. Covenants are early-warning tripwires, and watching them is central to credit monitoring.
- **Ingesting ongoing updates** — borrower reports, financials, filings, news, amendments — a continuous stream of mostly-unstructured documents (post 7) that must be read and reconciled against expectations.
- **Detecting variance and early warnings** — spotting *deviation from the thesis* early enough to act (restructure, hedge, exit) rather than discovering a problem at default.

The difficulty: monitoring is *continuous, voluminous, and unglamorous*. A portfolio of many positions generates a constant flood of updates, each requiring someone to read it and assess "does this change our view?" Traditionally this is *periodic* (quarterly reviews) and *manual*, so problems that emerge between reviews go unnoticed, and the sheer volume means updates get skimmed or missed. The result is the classic failure: *problems noticed too late*, when options are fewer and losses larger. Monitoring is where attention naturally lapses after the exciting underwriting decision — exactly the gap AI fills.

## How AI makes monitoring continuous

AI's tireless reading transforms monitoring from periodic-and-manual to continuous-and-automated, applying the document-reading capability (post 7) to the ongoing stream:

- **Continuous ingestion and reading.** Instead of a human periodically reviewing each position, AI *continuously reads* every incoming update (financials, filings, news, borrower reports) across the whole portfolio — something humans can't do at scale. Every update is actually read, not skimmed or queued, which closes the between-reviews gap where problems hide.
- **Automated covenant tracking.** Extract the covenants and their thresholds (from the documents, post 7), track the relevant metrics from each update, and *continuously compute headroom and flag approaching or actual breaches*. This turns covenant monitoring from a manual quarterly check into a continuous automated tripwire — a direct, high-value application.
- **Variance analysis against the thesis.** The key capability: compare each update against the *original underwriting thesis and expectations* (post 8), and flag *deviation* — the borrower missed projections, leverage rose beyond the thesis, a risk factor materialized. By anchoring to the captured underwriting judgment (post 12), the AI can continuously ask "does this still match what we underwrote?" and surface divergence early.
- **Early-warning signals.** Surface subtle deteriorating signals — softening language in filings, negative news, sector stress, patterns across similar positions — that a periodic manual review would miss, giving earlier warning when action still has value.
- **Escalation with ownership.** When something crosses a threshold, flag it, route it to the responsible owner, and log the escalation — turning detection into tracked action rather than a buried alert.

The transformation: monitoring becomes *continuous* (every update read as it arrives), *comprehensive* (every position, every covenant, all the time), and *proactive* (variance flagged early), with humans focused on *investigating and acting on* what the AI surfaces rather than on the reading-and-tracking drudgery. This directly attacks the "noticed too late" failure — the whole value is catching deterioration earlier, when there's still time to do something about it.

## Engineering it right — signal, noise, and accountability

Continuous monitoring introduces its own challenges that the engineering must handle, or it fails in predictable ways:

- **Signal vs noise (the central challenge).** Continuous monitoring can generate a flood of flags, and if most are noise, humans tune them out (alert fatigue) and miss the real ones — the same failure mode as noisy observability (the [observability](/blog/series/observability-engineering/) and [LLMOps](/blog/series/llmops-in-practice/) series). So the engineering must *prioritize*: surface what genuinely matters, suppress the noise, and tie flags to materiality. A monitoring system that cries wolf is worse than useless. Getting the signal-to-noise right is the make-or-break of monitoring.
- **Anchor to the thesis and keep it current.** Variance analysis is only as good as the *captured thesis* it compares against (posts 8, 12), so the underwriting judgment must be captured in a usable form, and the thesis updated as circumstances legitimately change (not every deviation is bad — some are expected). The monitoring must know what it's monitoring *against*.
- **Traceability and auditability (again).** Every flag and assessment must trace to its source (the update, the covenant, the thesis point) — post 7's provenance — so humans can verify it and the monitoring record is auditable. "The AI flagged this" must be backed by "here's the update and the covenant it relates to."
- **Human ownership of the response.** AI *detects and surfaces*; humans *decide what to do* (investigate, restructure, hedge, exit) and own the response (post 1). The AI makes sure nothing is missed and surfaces it early; the judgment about *how to respond* stays human. Escalation must have clear ownership so flags become tracked actions, not orphaned alerts.
- **Portfolio-level rollup.** Individual-position monitoring feeds the portfolio view (post 10) — concentrations of deterioration, correlated stress across positions, aggregate exposure to an emerging risk — so monitoring isn't just per-position but informs the whole book.

Monitoring is the long, unglamorous, high-value aftermath of every deal, and AI's tireless continuous reading transforms it from periodic-and-manual (problems noticed too late) to continuous-and-proactive (deterioration caught early). The engineering centers on *signal over noise* (or humans tune it out), anchoring to the *captured thesis*, keeping it *traceable and auditable*, and keeping *humans owning the response* while the AI ensures nothing is missed. Done right, monitoring is where AI financial engineering delivers some of its clearest value — not predicting markets (post 5–6's hard problem) but tirelessly watching what you already hold, so you find out about trouble while you can still do something about it.

The throughline: **monitoring** — the years-long surveillance after a deal, tracking whether the underwriting **thesis** still holds via **performance tracking**, **covenant compliance** (headroom and breach tripwires), **ingesting ongoing updates**, and **variance/early-warning detection** — is continuous, voluminous, and unglamorous, so traditionally it's *periodic and manual*, causing the classic failure: **problems noticed too late**. **AI makes it continuous**: tireless **ingestion/reading** of every update across the portfolio (closing the between-reviews gap), **automated covenant tracking** (continuous headroom/breach flags), **variance analysis against the captured thesis** (post 8/12 — "does this still match what we underwrote?"), **early-warning signals**, and **escalation with ownership** — shifting humans from reading-drudgery to investigating-and-acting, directly attacking "noticed too late." Engineer it around **signal over noise** (or alert fatigue kills it — the make-or-break), **anchoring to a current captured thesis**, **traceability/auditability** (every flag sourced), **human ownership of the response** (AI detects, humans decide), and **portfolio-level rollup** (post 10). It's where AI delivers clear value — not predicting markets but tirelessly watching what you hold.

## Key takeaways

- **Monitoring** is the long (years-long) aftermath of a deal: tracking whether the underwriting **thesis** (post 8) still holds via **performance vs thesis**, **covenant compliance** (watching **headroom** and approaching breaches — covenants are early-warning tripwires), **ingesting ongoing updates** (financials/filings/news/amendments — mostly unstructured, post 7), and **variance/early-warning detection**.
- It's traditionally **periodic and manual** against a **continuous, voluminous** stream, so problems emerging between reviews go unnoticed and updates get skimmed — causing the classic failure, **problems noticed too late** (fewer options, larger losses); attention naturally lapses after the exciting underwriting decision.
- **AI makes it continuous**: tireless **ingestion/reading** of every update across the whole portfolio (closes the between-reviews gap), **automated covenant tracking** (continuously compute headroom, flag breaches), **variance analysis against the captured thesis** (anchor to the underwriting judgment, posts 8/12 — "does this still match what we underwrote?"), **subtle early-warning signals** (softening filing language, negative news, sector/peer stress), and **escalation with ownership** — shifting humans from reading-drudgery to investigating and acting, directly attacking "noticed too late."
- Engineer around the central challenge **signal vs noise**: a monitor that floods humans with low-value flags causes **alert fatigue** and misses the real ones (as in observability/LLMOps) — prioritize by materiality, suppress noise (a monitor that cries wolf is worse than useless); also **anchor to a current captured thesis** (not every deviation is bad), keep it **traceable/auditable** (every flag sourced, post 7), and keep **humans owning the response** (AI detects/surfaces, humans decide and act; escalations have clear ownership).
- Individual monitoring **rolls up to the portfolio view** (post 10 — concentrations of deterioration, correlated stress, aggregate emerging-risk exposure); done right, monitoring is where AI delivers some of its **clearest finance value** — not predicting markets (posts 5–6) but tirelessly watching what you already hold so trouble is found while you can still act.
