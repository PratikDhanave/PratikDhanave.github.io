# The PreSales / Sales Engineer Role

*There's a job that most engineers have never heard of and that many would actually enjoy: the sales engineer, or presales engineer — the technical expert who partners with salespeople to win technical deals. It's where deep technical knowledge meets customer-facing work, where you help customers solve real problems with technology rather than writing code all day, and where an engineer who can also communicate becomes genuinely rare and valuable. If you've ever wondered how technical products actually get sold to technical buyers, this role is a big part of the answer.*

**Presales** — and the **sales engineer** (SE) / solutions engineer role at its center — is where engineers most directly participate in sales. This post covers what presales is, what a sales engineer does, why the role is so valuable (and a great fit for many engineers), and the skills it requires. It's the most engineer-relevant post in the series, because it's a genuine technical career path within sales — and understanding it illuminates how technical selling works.

## What presales is

**Presales** refers to the activities that support a sale *before* it closes — specifically the *technical* work of helping a prospect understand, evaluate, and validate that a product will solve their problem. It exists because technical products need technical selling:

- **It's the technical side of selling.** For complex/technical products, the sales process involves substantial *technical* work: answering deep technical questions, demonstrating the product, designing how it would fit the customer's environment, running proofs of concept, and addressing technical concerns. This technical selling work is *presales*, and it's typically done by *sales engineers* (technical people) partnering with salespeople. Presales is where the technical substance of the sale happens.
- **It complements the salesperson.** In technical B2B sales, a common model pairs a *salesperson* (who owns the relationship, commercial process, and closing) with a *sales engineer* (who owns the technical evaluation — demos, technical questions, POCs, solution design). The salesperson handles the business side; the SE handles the technical side. Together they sell technical products to technical buyers — the salesperson can't answer deep technical questions credibly, and the engineer isn't running the commercial process, so they partner.
- **It's essential because technical buyers demand technical validation.** Technical buyers won't adopt on a salesperson's word — they need to *technically evaluate* the product (does it actually work, integrate, scale, solve their problem?). Presales provides that technical credibility and validation. Without it, technical sales stalls at "we need to talk to someone technical." Presales is how technical products earn technical buyers' confidence.

Presales is the technical side of selling — helping prospects technically understand, evaluate, and validate a product — done by sales engineers partnering with salespeople, and essential because technical buyers require technical validation. At its center is a role many engineers would find appealing: the sales engineer.

## What a sales engineer does

The **sales engineer** (SE), or solutions engineer, is a technical person in a customer-facing sales role — combining engineering knowledge with communication. Their core responsibilities:

- **Technical discovery and solution design.** SEs deeply understand the customer's *technical* situation and problem (technical discovery), and design/explain how the product would fit and solve it (solution design/architecture). They're the technical diagnostician and architect in the sale — figuring out how the product applies to *this* customer's technical reality.
- **Demos and technical presentations.** SEs deliver *product demonstrations* tailored to the customer's needs — showing, technically and credibly, how the product solves their specific problem (the demos post covers this). A good technical demo, run by someone who genuinely understands both the product and the customer, is one of the most persuasive things in technical sales.
- **Answering technical questions and objections.** SEs field the deep technical questions and concerns that technical buyers raise (integration, security, scale, architecture) — credibly, because they actually understand the technology. They're the trusted technical voice that reassures technical buyers, which a non-technical salesperson can't be.
- **Proofs of concept (POCs) and technical evaluation.** SEs often run *proofs of concept* — helping the customer test the product in their own environment to validate it works for them (the POC post). This hands-on technical validation is frequently decisive in technical deals, and the SE guides it.
- **Bridging customer and product.** SEs also carry technical customer feedback back to the product/engineering team (what customers need, where the product falls short) — a valuable bridge between the market and the product (like product marketing, but technical and deal-driven).

The sales engineer is the technical expert in the sale — doing technical discovery, solution design, demos, answering technical questions, running POCs, and bridging to product. It's a role where deep technical knowledge is applied to help customers, in a customer-facing (not code-writing) way. For engineers who enjoy understanding technology *and* working with people and problems, it's a genuinely appealing path.

## Why the role is valuable (and a great fit for engineers)

The sales engineer role is highly valued and often an excellent fit for technically-minded people who also like communicating — worth understanding as a career possibility:

- **The combination is rare and valuable.** People who *both* have deep technical knowledge *and* can communicate well with customers are relatively rare — most people are strong at one or the other. Sales engineers sit at that valuable intersection, which makes the role well-compensated and in demand. If you're an engineer who can also explain and empathize (the EQ series' skills), you're exactly the profile SEs need.
- **It's technical work with people and impact.** SEs use their technical skills *while* working with customers, solving real problems, and directly influencing whether deals (and revenue) happen. For engineers who find pure coding isolating or want more customer contact, variety, and visible business impact, it offers technical depth *plus* human interaction and clear impact. Many engineers who try it find they love it.
- **It's a genuine technical career path in sales.** Presales/SE is a real, respected career — not "failed engineer" or "just sales," but a distinct discipline requiring genuine technical expertise. It can be as intellectually demanding as engineering (understanding products deeply, solving customers' technical problems) while adding the customer-facing dimension. It's also often a path toward broader roles (product, leadership, founding) because it builds both technical and commercial understanding.
- **It demystifies sales for engineers.** Understanding this role reveals that technical sales *depends on engineers* — that selling technical products is substantially a technical activity, done by technical people. This should further dissolve the engineer's disdain for sales: technical selling is engineering-adjacent work, done with technical integrity, helping customers solve real problems.

The sales engineer role is valuable because it combines rare skills (technical depth + communication), offers technical work with people and impact, is a genuine respected career path, and shows how central engineers are to technical sales. For the right person, it's an appealing alternative or complement to pure engineering — and understanding it reframes sales as engineering-adjacent, not alien.

## The skills a sales engineer needs

Finally, the skills the SE role requires — a blend that many engineers can develop:

- **Deep technical knowledge.** The foundation — genuinely understanding the product and the relevant technology well enough to answer hard questions, design solutions, and earn technical buyers' trust. This is the engineering half, and it's non-negotiable (technical buyers detect faked expertise instantly, like the developer-marketing post).
- **Communication and translation.** The ability to *communicate* technical concepts clearly — to technical buyers (credibly, in depth) and sometimes to less-technical stakeholders (translating value) — and to *present* and *demo* effectively. This is the skill many engineers must develop, and it's the differentiator (deep knowledge you can't communicate isn't enough in this role).
- **Customer empathy and listening.** Understanding the customer's problem and needs (discovery, from the previous post — diagnosis), which requires empathy and active listening (the EQ series). SEs must genuinely understand customers to design solutions and demos that fit — the same understand-before-selling principle.
- **Problem-solving applied to customers.** Applying engineering problem-solving to the *customer's* problem — figuring out how the product solves their specific situation, designing the fit, troubleshooting in POCs. This is engineering skill aimed at customer problems, which is deeply natural for engineers.
- **Trustworthiness and honesty.** Because SEs are the trusted technical voice, *honesty* is essential — technical buyers trust an SE who tells the truth (including product limitations) and distrust one who overclaims. The honest-selling principle (from post one) is especially vital for the technical expert in the room. An SE's credibility is their core asset.

The presales / sales engineer role is where engineers most directly do sales — the technical expert who partners with salespeople to help customers understand, evaluate, and validate technical products, through discovery, solution design, demos, technical answers, and POCs. It's a valuable, appealing career path combining rare skills (technical depth + communication), and it reframes technical sales as engineering-adjacent work done with integrity. ## Key takeaways

- Presales is the technical side of selling — the work of helping prospects technically understand, evaluate, and validate that a product solves their problem — done by sales engineers partnering with salespeople (SE owns the technical side, salesperson owns the commercial), essential because technical buyers require technical validation, not a salesperson's word.
- The sales engineer (SE) / solutions engineer does technical discovery and solution design, delivers tailored demos and technical presentations, answers deep technical questions and objections credibly, runs proofs of concept (hands-on validation), and bridges customer feedback to product — deep technical knowledge applied in a customer-facing (not code-writing) way.
- The role is valuable and often a great fit for engineers because the combination of deep technical knowledge *and* good communication is rare (well-compensated, in demand), it offers technical work with people/impact/variety, it's a genuine respected career path (and a route toward product/leadership/founding), and it demystifies sales by showing how central engineers are to technical selling.
- The skills it needs are a developable blend: deep technical knowledge (non-negotiable — faked expertise is detected instantly), communication/translation (the differentiator many engineers must develop), customer empathy and listening (diagnosis), engineering problem-solving aimed at the customer's problem, and trustworthiness/honesty (credibility is the SE's core asset).
- Understanding this role should further dissolve the engineer's disdain for sales: technical selling is substantially a *technical* activity done by technical people with integrity, helping customers solve real problems — engineering-adjacent work, not alien.
