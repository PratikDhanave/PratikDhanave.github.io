Conditioning and Classifier-Free Guidance — How Text Steers the Image

Everything so far generates an image — any image the training distribution contains. A product needs the image you asked for. Conditioning is how a prompt gets wired into every denoising step, and classifier-free guidance — a two-line trick discovered almost apologetically — is the single knob that most determines whether the output actually obeys the prompt. The famous "CFG scale" slider in every image UI is this post.

Conditioning wires the prompt into every denoising step via text encoders and cross-attention — but the honest conditional distribution follows prompts too loosely. Classifier-free guidance fixes it: co-train an unconditional mode, then extrapolate the difference at sampling. One knob for fidelity vs diversity, 2× cost, and negative prompts for free.

The Label Problem — Delay, Noise, and the Data Your Own Decisions Hide

Supervised learning assumes someone hands you the truth. In fraud, the truth arrives 30 to 90 days late, labeled by processes with their own agendas, for only the transactions you approved — and silence means nothing, because unreported fraud and un-disputed legitimate purchases are indistinguishable until they aren't. Label engineering is the least glamorous layer of a fraud system and the one that decides whether everything upstream learns reality or a convenient fiction.

Labels are an engineered pipeline: maturity horizons (recent silence is not legitimacy), labels as versioned state trajectories, early proxies weighted then reconciled; noise handled by provenance; and censoring confronted with bought data — bounded exploration of would-be declines booked as data acquisition cost.

Test Environments — From the Staging Queue to Ephemeral Everything

Every scaling organization hits the same wall: one shared staging environment, N teams queuing for it, test runs colliding in it, and a database whose state is the accumulated sediment of every experiment ever run there. "It passed in staging" slowly loses all meaning. The way out isn't a better staging — it's dissolving the singleton: environments that are created per purpose, seeded to a known state, isolated from each other, and destroyed without ceremony.

The staging singleton fails by structure: contention, interference, accreted state, unowned fidelity decay. The cure is ephemeral environments — declared, purpose-scoped, seeded to known state, isolated by construction, created in minutes, reaped by TTL — paired honestly with canaries and flags for what only production reveals.

The Memory Hierarchy of an Agent

Cognitive science spent a century mapping human memory into distinct systems — working, episodic, semantic, procedural — and agent engineering has been rediscovering the same partition from the opposite direction, because the same pressures produce the same shapes. The hierarchy isn't biomimicry for its own sake: each memory type answers a different question, is stored differently, retrieved differently, and fails differently. Confusing them is the root of most memory-system design mistakes.

Agent memory decomposes into four types with different jobs: working (the context window), episodic (what happened — read as history), semantic (what is true — needs revision, not just insertion), and procedural (how to act — evolves like code). They differ in storage, retrieval, and lifecycle, and most memory bugs are type confusions.

Latent Diffusion — Why Stable Diffusion Doesn't Diffuse Pixels

The single decision most responsible for putting image generation on consumer GPUs was not a better denoiser or a faster sampler — it was moving the entire diffusion process out of pixel space. Latent diffusion compresses images roughly 48× with a learned autoencoder, runs every denoising step in that compressed space, and decodes once at the end. Same theory as posts 2–5, radically different economics: this is the architecture decision that made Stable Diffusion stable to run.

Stable Diffusion doesn't diffuse pixels. A perceptually-trained autoencoder compresses images ~48×, the entire diffusion apparatus runs unchanged on latents, and a decoder re-synthesizes texture once at the end. The economics, the three-piece architecture, the autoencoder-as-quality-ceiling caveat, and why video inherited the pattern.

Reconciliation and Durable Sync — The Backstop That Makes Webhooks Trustworthy

Everything in this series hardened the event path — and post 1's contract still stands: delivery is not guaranteed, eventually. Endpoints stay down past retry horizons, subscriptions get misconfigured, providers have incidents too, and events from before your integration existed never arrive at all. The final discipline accepts this and closes the loop: periodic reconciliation against the source of truth, convergence-oriented sync design, and the backfill machinery that makes "we missed a week" a procedure instead of a crisis. This is what turns a webhook integration into a synchronization system.

Non-delivery is in the contract, so build the backstop: modified-since sweeps through the same version-gated upserts (healing and counting missed deliveries), deliberate deletion detection, and rehearsed resumable backfill. The end state is a sync engine: events for latency, reconciliation for truth, replay for recovery.

Scoring, Thresholds, and the Cost of Errors — Where ML Meets the P&L

A fraud model outputs a probability; the business needs a decision; and the line between them — the threshold — is where machine learning stops and economics begins. Set it by gut and you've made your most consequential business decision by vibes. This post builds the machinery that makes the error trade-off explicit: metrics that survive extreme imbalance, calibration that makes scores mean something, and thresholds derived from costs rather than from tradition.

At 0.1% base rates ROC-AUC flatters; use precision at review capacity and recall at FP budget on time-split, segmented data. Calibration makes scores probabilities; thresholds become cost derivations that move with amount and segment, extended into a ladder across approve, friction, review, and decline.

Flakiness — The Operational Disease That Kills Test Signal

A flaky test — same code, different verdicts — is worse than no test: it costs full price, delivers noise, and teaches the team that red means retry. Post 1 did the arithmetic (ten thousand 99.9%-reliable tests ≈ permanent red); this post is the operating system for the disease: detect flakes statistically, quarantine them mechanically, fix them by root-cause class, and prevent the re-infection that follows any one-time cleanup.

Flakiness managed as an operational disease: detected statistically into per-test flake scores, quarantined mechanically (out of the gate, loudly, with fix-or-delete SLAs), fixed by taxonomy — synchronization over sleeps, hermetic isolation, fakes at boundaries — with lint rules and burn-in preventing re-infection.
