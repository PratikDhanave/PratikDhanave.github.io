Building a Great Team

Everything in this series — hiring, interviewing, onboarding, feedback, growth, retention — converges on a single goal: building a great team. A great team isn't just a collection of talented individuals; it's the right people, working well together, in an environment that lets them do their best work. This closing post pulls the threads together into the bigger picture of team-building, and returns to where the series began: because an organization is its people, building a great team is arguably the most important and highest-leverage thing any leader can do.

This final post synthesizes the series into building a great team — the goal all the people practices serve. It covers what makes a great team (beyond individual talent), the role of culture and diversity, people-first leadership, and a synthesis of the series. It ties hiring, onboarding, feedback, growth, and retention together into the bigger picture: building and sustaining a great team, which — since people are everything — is among the most important things a leader does.

What makes a great team

A great team is more than a collection of talented individuals — it’s the right people, working well together, in an environment that lets them excel:

  • Not just talented individuals. A great team isn’t merely a group of talented people — talented individuals who don’t work well together (or in a bad environment) underperform. A great team is about the right people working well together well-supported, not just individual talent summed up. Great teams are more than talented individuals. Chemistry, not just talent.
  • The right people, working well together. A great team combines the right people (skilled, good fit — hiring) working well together (collaboration, trust, complementary strengths, psychological safety — the EQ/ops themes) — the combination and collaboration matter as much as individual ability. A great team gels (works well together), not just staffs (has good people). Right people, working well together. Collaboration matters as much as talent.
  • In an enabling environment. A great team needs an environment that lets people do their best work — good culture (the ops series), psychological safety (the EQ series), support, growth, and good management (this series). The environment (culture, support, leadership) enables (or hinders) the team’s greatness. Even great people need an enabling environment. Environment enables greatness. The context matters.

A great team is more than talented individuals — it’s the right people (hiring), working well together (collaboration, trust, psychological safety), in an enabling environment (good culture, support, management). The combination — right people, gelling well, well-supported — is what makes a team great. And two elements deserve emphasis: culture and diversity.

Culture and diversity

Two elements are especially important to a great team — culture (the environment) and diversity (varied perspectives) — worth emphasizing:

  • Culture shapes the team. Culture (the ops series — shared norms and behaviors) profoundly shapes the team — a healthy culture (trust, psychological safety, growth, respect) enables a great team; a toxic one undermines it (even with talented people). Building and protecting a good culture is central to a great team (and shaped by actions — the ops post). Culture makes or breaks the team. A healthy environment enables greatness.
  • Diversity strengthens teams. Diversity (varied backgrounds, perspectives, experiences) strengthens teams — diverse perspectives improve problem-solving, decisions, and innovation (avoiding groupthink, seeing more angles). A diverse team (with an inclusive environment where diverse people thrive) is generally stronger than a homogeneous one. Diversity strengthens teams. Varied perspectives help.
  • Inclusion makes diversity work. Diversity alone isn’t enough — an inclusive environment (where diverse people feel they belong and can contribute) is needed to realize diversity’s benefits. Diversity plus inclusion (varied people who thrive) is what strengthens teams. Diversity needs inclusion. Belonging realizes diversity’s value.
  • Avoiding the “culture fit” bias. Recall (the interviewing post): vague “culture fit” often masks bias and reduces diversity (hiring people “like us”). Building a diverse team means hiring on job-relevant merit (not similarity) and seeking “culture add” (what someone brings) over “culture fit” (are they like us). Hire for culture add, not narrow fit. Add, not just fit. Diversity over sameness.

Culture (a healthy environment — trust, psychological safety, growth) and diversity (varied perspectives, with inclusion so diverse people thrive) are especially important to a great team — culture enables it, diversity strengthens it (avoiding the “culture fit” bias that reduces diversity, seeking “culture add” instead). These, with the right people gelling well, make a great team. Building it takes a people-first approach.

People-first leadership

Building a great team requires people-first leadership — leaders who genuinely prioritize and invest in their people:

  • Prioritize people. Building a great team requires leaders who prioritize people — treating hiring, developing, and retaining people as central (not secondary to “real work”), because the people are the work (the first post). People-first leaders invest in their team (hiring well, developing, retaining, good management). Prioritize people as central. People are the priority.
  • It’s the leader’s highest-leverage work. For leaders, building and developing the team is arguably the highest-leverage work (from the first post — people are everything) — a great leader’s biggest impact is often the team they build and develop (which then does great things). Investing in people is a leader’s highest-return activity. Team-building is a leader’s highest-leverage work. Your team is your biggest impact.
  • It draws on all the people skills. People-first leadership draws on everything — hiring well (this series), the EQ skills (empathy, feedback, influence, psychological safety — the EQ series), good management, and genuinely caring about people. Building a great team is the culmination of the people and EQ skills. Team-building uses all the people skills. The culmination of people skills.
  • Caring about people is central. Underlying it all, genuinely caring about people (their success, growth, wellbeing) is central to people-first leadership — people can tell if leaders genuinely care, and it profoundly affects engagement, retention, and team greatness. Genuine care isn’t soft; it’s central to building great teams. Genuinely care about people. Care is central, not soft.

Building a great team requires people-first leadership — leaders who prioritize and genuinely invest in their people (recognizing it as their highest-leverage work), drawing on all the people and EQ skills, and genuinely caring. It’s the culmination of taking people seriously. This synthesizes the whole series.

The series in summary

To close, a synthesis of hiring and people:

  • The people lifecycle. The series covered the people lifecycle — hiring (finding and selecting the right people — the process, interviewing/assessment), onboarding (getting them productive and integrated), developing (feedback, growth, career development), and retaining (keeping them engaged and staying) — the full arc of building and sustaining a great team. The whole people lifecycle. Hire, onboard, develop, retain.
  • The recurring themes. Throughout: people are the highest-leverage area (an org is its people); do it deliberately (hiring as a process, structured assessment, deliberate onboarding/development/retention — not ad-hoc); growth and retention are linked; good management and genuine care matter enormously; and the EQ skills (empathy, feedback, influence, psychological safety) underpin it all. People, deliberately, with care. The through-lines.
  • It all builds a great team. Everything serves building and sustaining a great team — the right people, working well together, in an enabling environment (culture, diversity, growth, good management). A great team is the goal and the payoff of all the people practices. It all builds a great team. The convergent goal.
  • Because people are everything. The series began and ends here: an organization is its people, so building a great team is arguably the most important and highest-leverage thing a leader does. Taking people seriously — hiring, developing, and keeping great people well — is how organizations (and leaders) truly succeed. People are everything, so team-building matters most. The bottom line.

Building a great team — the right people, working well together, in an enabling environment (culture, diversity, growth, good management) — is the goal all the people practices serve, requiring people-first leadership and genuine care. That completes the series: from why people are everything, through hiring, interviewing, onboarding, feedback, growth, and retention, to building a great team. Because an organization is its people, building and sustaining a great team is arguably the most important, highest-leverage thing any leader can do.

Key takeaways

  • A great team is more than a collection of talented individuals — it’s the right people (hiring), working well together (collaboration, trust, psychological safety — talented people who don’t gel underperform), in an enabling environment (good culture, support, management); the combination and collaboration matter as much as individual ability.
  • Culture and diversity are especially important: a healthy culture (trust, psychological safety, growth) enables a great team while a toxic one undermines it, and diversity (varied perspectives) strengthens teams (better problem-solving, less groupthink) — but needs inclusion (diverse people who thrive) to realize its benefits, and hiring for “culture add” over narrow “culture fit” (which masks bias and reduces diversity).
  • Building a great team requires people-first leadership — leaders who prioritize and genuinely invest in their people (recognizing it as their highest-leverage work, since people are everything), draw on all the people and EQ skills (empathy, feedback, influence, psychological safety), and genuinely care (which people can tell, and which is central, not soft).
  • The series covered the full people lifecycle — hiring (process, interviewing/assessment), onboarding (getting people productive/integrated), developing (feedback, growth, career development), and retaining (engagement, keeping people) — all done deliberately (as processes, not ad-hoc), with recurring themes of people-as-highest-leverage, growth-retention linkage, good management, and genuine care.
  • It all builds and sustains a great team (the goal and payoff of the people practices) — and because an organization is its people, building a great team is arguably the most important, highest-leverage thing a leader can do, so taking people seriously (hiring, developing, keeping great people well) is how organizations and leaders truly succeed.
