# The FDE Craft: People, Practice & Influence

*Everything technical in this field guide runs on something human: the ability to work with stakeholders, grow in the role, build a team, and shape a community. This theme is the craft and the career — the part that doesn't show up in a repo but decides whether the rest works. This post maps it.*

Fifth in [The Forward Deployed Field Guide](/blog/posts/fdfg-01-understanding-customers-and-driving-ai-adoption.html). The other themes are what a forward-deployed engineer *does*; this one is what makes them effective at it over time — the people skills, the career, and the influence that compounds beyond any single engagement.

## The topics in this theme

- **Stakeholders.** Forward-deployed work is relentlessly cross-functional — the champion, the skeptic, the budget-holder, the engineer whose code you're touching, the security reviewer. Reading the room, building trust fast, and navigating competing interests is a core, learnable skill.
- **The role and the spectrum.** What a forward-deployed engineer actually is, and how the role sits on the technical-GTM spectrum from sales engineer to implementation. Understanding the role clearly is how you defend and grow it.
- **Careers.** How an engineer grows into confident customer-facing work, and the paths forward — senior FDE, forward-deployed architect, forward-deployed leader, or independent practice.
- **Hiring.** What makes a good forward-deployed engineer (builder + consultant + communicator), how to interview for the blend, and how to build a team that isn't just strong engineers who dislike customers.
- **Mentoring and coaching.** Growing the next engineers into the role — and, in an engagement, coaching the customer's team so the capability stays.
- **Community and influence.** Writing, speaking, and contributing — the influence that builds a reputation and a practice, and that advances the discipline for everyone.

## The new AI topics reshaping it

- **The role is exploding.** AI made forward deployment mandatory (a capable model still needs someone to make it work for the customer), so FDE and solutions-engineering roles are proliferating at AI companies — which raises fresh questions about hiring, leveling, and career paths that the field is working out in real time.
- **Builder-communicators are the scarce resource.** The blend AI companies need — people who can both build agentic systems and sit with a customer — is rare, which reshapes hiring and makes mentoring into the role a strategic priority.
- **Community as infrastructure.** A practitioner community (meetups, writing, shared patterns) is forming around the role, and participating in it is becoming part of the craft.

## Why the human craft decides the outcome

A forward-deployed engineer can have perfect technical judgment and still fail if the skeptical engineer routes around them, the champion loses faith, or the team can't carry what was built. Conversely, strong relationships make everything else easier — the team tells you the truth about the data, lets you make the hard call, and adopts what you build. This theme is the medium through which all the others flow; it's why "soft skills" is the wrong name for the skills that most determine whether an engagement succeeds.
