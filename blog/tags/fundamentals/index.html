What Financial Engineering Is

Financial engineering is what happens when you point the tools of mathematics, computer science, and engineering at money. It is the discipline behind pricing a derivative, measuring the risk of a portfolio, and deciding what a future cash flow is worth today. For engineers, it is a surprisingly familiar world — models, assumptions, edge cases, and a relentless question of what a thing is really worth. This series builds it from first principles.

Financial engineering applies math, statistics, and computation to money — and under the jargon it rests on three ideas an engineer will recognize: the time value of money, the trade-off between risk and return, and no-arbitrage. This series builds quant finance from those foundations. (Educational, not financial advice.)

Q-Learning and Temporal-Difference Learning — Learning Value from Experience

The elegant idea of value functions becomes a practical algorithm through one trick: learn by comparing your prediction to a slightly-better prediction, and nudge toward the difference. Temporal-difference learning and its famous instance, Q-learning, are how an agent learns the value of its actions purely from experience — no model of the world required. This is the algorithm that first made reinforcement learning work, and its core idea quietly underlies much of the field.

Learn a guess from a better guess: temporal-difference learning and Q-learning nudge value estimates toward reward + γ × value-of-next, model-free, bootstrapping from every step. Off-policy learning, ε-greedy exploration, and why the Q-table can't scale — motivating deep RL.
